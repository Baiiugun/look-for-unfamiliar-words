URBAN TRANSPORT: PROBLEMS AND SOLUTIONS

1 Getting around today's cities can be a challenge. Many modern cities have terrible traffic congestion. It can take hours to cross the city because there are simply too many cars. Governments and businesses around the world are finding creative ways to solve these problems.

2 In cities like Jakarta, Indonesia, commuters may spend four hours in their cars every day. The solution? Add modern technology to the familiar motorcycle. Because they are small, motorcycles can fit into small spaces and move around cars that are stopped in traffic. Motorcycle owners can earn money by taking passengers to their destinations. In 2010, a new company began to offer motorcycle rides as an online service using an app. Now Jakarta residents can use their phones to call a motorcycle. It's cheap, safe, reliable and most importantly - fast.

3 Congestion is not the only transport problem in big cities, however. Building roads is expensive. Building a public transport system is even more expensive. As a result, communities on the outskirts of major cities often have few roads. The challenge is even greater when these communities are high up in the mountains. Without public transport, it is difficult for residents to reach the city centre. They don't have access to jobs, education or healthcare.

4 A good way to connect mountain communities to the city centre is a cable car system, which operates high above traffic. Because they use electricity, cable cars are cleaner than cars and buses that use petrol. They are also less expensive to build than buses or trains. The cars don't go very fast - only about 20 km per hour - but that is often faster than the traffic on the roads below.

5 For people who live in mountain communities with few roads, cable cars make a big difference. One teacher in Bogotá, Colombia reported that it used to take him more than two hours to get to work, often arriving at the school after his students! With the cable car, he can be there in 40 minutes. Cable cars can also bring unexpected changes. Although they are built for residents, tourists love cable cars. In cities like Medellín, Colombia and La Paz, Bolivia, they are bringing in tourists to areas of the city that were too far away, or some cases, considered too dangerous for visitors. Now tourists visit these areas, where they spend money in shops and restaurants.

6 Finally, the best way to get around a city with bad traffic is often walking. But walking in a city on a mountain is hard work. Another creative solution is the outdoor escalator. In Medellín, residents once had to climb uphill for a half an hour (= 28 stories!) to their homes. Today, the escalator ride takes just six minutes. The world's longest outdoor elevator is in Hong Kong. It is 800 m and serves almost 80,000 people every day, including many tourists.

7 Traffic congestion will continue to be a problem in big cities. However, creative solutions like these can help people get around cities more easily. They can also reduce pollution and traffic and attract tourists.
