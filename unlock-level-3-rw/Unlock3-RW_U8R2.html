WHAT HAS HAPPENED TO THE DREAM OF A BETTER LIFE?

1 In almost every country and every culture, parents work hard to try to give their children a better life than they had. For decades following the Second World War, this dream became a reality in many countries around the world. Possibly the best example is the Japanese "economic miracle." Thanks to a high rate of economic growth from the 1960s to the 1980s, it developed into one of the world's strongest economies. Yet in Japan, as in other developed countries, many people today are not able to enjoy a higher, or even the same standard of living as their parents. What has happened? Why do many people feel poorer than they were 10 or 20 years ago?

2 The answer is complicated, and every country tells a different story. The world's largest economy, the United States, provides a good example. One key factor which influences the standard of living is expenditures, which is the money you spend on the things that you need. From 2000 until 2015, Americans spent approximately 20% of their incomes on housing, about 10% on food, about 8% on transport, and around 3% each on entertainment and health. But these costs have been rising steadily. They now must spend about 34% on housing and 13% on food.

3 In the United States, as in many other countries, the expenditures that are rising the fastest are housing and healthcare. In many cities worldwide, there is a serious shortage of houses and apartments to buy or rent. Healthcare costs also continue to rise as medicine and hospital care have become more expensive. Most recently, there have also been significant increases in food prices.

4 However, expenditures are only half the picture. The other half is the money people earn, their income. In the United States, in general, incomes have also gone up (see Figure1). Yet not all incomes are increasing at the same rate. For many people, they have not increased as much as expenditures. This means that their "buying power" has decreased. Figure2 shows this increase in inequality. It shows change in income by group, from the bottom 20% to the top 20%. The top line shows the highest 5%. It is clear that the incomes of the higher income groups are rising the fastest. The incomes of the lower groups are rising more slowly. It is not surprising that they do not have a higher standard of living than their parents.

5 In conclusion, the impact of economic forces is not the same for everyone. In the United States, rising prices have had the greatest impact on the people with lower incomes. They must pay more for necessities like food, healthcare and especially housing, and as a result, they have less money for education, investment and savings. This makes it harder for the next generation to move up the economic ladder. For them, the dream of a better life than their parents is still only a dream.
